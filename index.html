Mill Creek Ravine · Edmonton

Mill Creek, 1967 and now

Mill Creek used to run all the way down its ravine to the North Saskatchewan. In 1970 its last stretch, from Connors Road to the river, was diverted to make room for roads. Drag the slider to see where it went.

1967Today

Left of the slider is a 1967 city street map; right of it is today's map. The dashed line traces the creek as the 1967 map drew it. The solid blue is the creek in Alberta's base mapping today, and the gap in it is the part that went underground. The orange line is the LRT route as planned in 2015; the line as built shows in grey on today's map. Use two fingers or the + and − buttons to zoom.

Three places to look

The mouth

Where it met the river

In 1967 the creek looped past 98 Avenue and out to the North Saskatchewan. Today about 150 m of channel near the river still carries water, cut off from the rest.

The gap

Where it goes under

Just south of Connors Road the creek disappears. Follow the dashed line north: about 900 m of the old channel has no creek in it now.

The tracks

The LRT on the creek bed

Between Connors Road and 98 Avenue the Valley Line runs right over the old channel. This map was first made in 2015 to show how close the planned route came to the lost creek. The line opened in 2023.

How it got here

  1. 1878

    William Bird, a Hudson's Bay Company employee, opens a flour mill near where the creek enters the river. The creek is named for it.

  2. 1960s–70s

    Neighbours and naturalists fight off a plan to run a freeway through the ravine. The ravine survives; the creek's lower end does not.

  3. 1970

    The last stretch of the creek, through the ravine under Connors Road and 98 Avenue to the river, is diverted and put underground.

  4. March 2015

    As the Valley Line LRT is designed through the bottom of the ravine, people push for it to leave room to bring the creek back. This map is first made.

  5. 2015 on

    The City studies daylighting the lower creek, bringing it back to the surface.

  6. Nov. 2023

    The Valley Line Southeast opens, with the Muttart stop beside the old creek bed.

  7. Aug. 2025

    A council committee recommends replacing the 76 Avenue culvert, upstream of this map, with a bridge, letting that stretch of creek run in the open.
